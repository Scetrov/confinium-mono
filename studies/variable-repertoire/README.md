# Confinium Mono Variable · Full Grid

[Variable TTF](fonts/ConfiniumMono-VariableRepertoire%5Bwght%5D.ttf) · [Thin, Book, Bold comparison](verification/full-grid-weights.png) · [Explorer](../../explorer.html)

Full Grid is the outline source for **every** glyph, including Q. At weight
700 outlines and horizontal metrics match Full Grid exactly: 800-unit cell,
800 cap height, 560 x-height, zero-width combining marks, 1040/−200 line
metrics. Weights 100–700 interpolate through Thin and Regular (400, previously Book) construction
masters. Most are generated by the general inset; M, N, and W use explicit
Thin constructions with parallel stroke edges and direct intersections.
Cyrillic М and the accented capitals based on W inherit those corrections.
The light-end n/m/p/q/r/u now share straight stem and bar zones, while w
and X use balanced parallel diagonals. Accented forms and shared Cyrillic
outlines inherit the relevant changes. The light-end a/b/d/z use aligned
lower bowls or bars, and g/j use matched descender-hook geometry. Bold and
the static styles are unchanged. Lowercase k and x use matched 54-unit
normal diagonal strokes at Thin, with flat endpoints. Lowercase y's upper
terminals also stay on the x-height. The regression suite checks that every
Latin lowercase glyph preserves its Bold alignment zones through the axis;
that check is not a measurement of every stroke in every letter. Extended
Latin and Cyrillic Thin masters use shared walls and bar zones for slashed
bowls, ligatures, thorn, and the Cyrillic soft-sign family; the diagonals in
И/Й/и/й use parallel edges. Accented N/M/T/f forms reuse their corrected
bases without changing the mark geometry. The same approach balances the
rounded U/C/O accent bases, the bars of Ð and ю, and the shared walls of
Œ and œ.

The historical experiment supplies axis and naming conventions only. Its 697-unit
sources, fonts and proofs remain archived. The earlier adapted variable
study's [raster](verification/weights.png) is retained for comparison.

### First optical pass, in priority order

1. **Book: separate `O` and `0`.** The faceted zero is too close to `O` at
   12–14 px in both themes; lowercase `o` already reads by height. Try making
   the existing zero facets or width contrast more apparent before adding a mark.
2. **Thin: even out stroke visibility.** At 12–14 px, braces, parentheses,
   and some fine strokes fade next to heavier marks and letters such as `Þ`.
   Normalize the thin end in context rather than thickening everything equally.
3. **Book: stress-test `Il1`.** The distinctions are easier to see at 16 px;
   verify them in unlabeled identifiers before deciding whether to adjust terminals.
   `rn/m`, underscore clearance, and accented capitals were not the first problems
   in this sample. Recheck them during regression review.

These are observations from Chromium at 100% zoom, not a transcription test
or a rendering guarantee across terminals and operating systems. No outlines
were changed for this pass.
